Pas De Nom is one of the most iconic teams in competitive HaxBall history, best known for its tactical innovation during the Flash era and its success in the HaxBall Champions League.

The club became closely associated with some of the most influential players of its era, including Kranjcar, Guti.Haz, and Tino, a striker whose individual quality made him one of the defining attackers of his generation. Pas De Nom also developed a distinctive tactical identity through the use of Dropball Jukes, turning the technique into a central part of its collective style.

Pas De Nom reached two HaxBall Champions League finals during the Flash era, capturing its first and only title in Edition #7 before making another deep run to the championship match in Edition #12.

Beyond its results, Pas De Nom's tactical identity, influential players, and memorable rivalry with Angels of Death established the team as an important part of early competitive HaxBall history.

Overview

Pas De Nom emerged as one of the notable teams of the early competitive HaxBall era. The club became associated with a group of highly skilled players and developed a distinctive tactical identity that would later become one of the most recognizable styles of the Flash era.

Among the players most closely connected with the team were Kranjcar, Guti.Haz, and Tino. Their individual quality, combined with Pas De Nom's tactical approach, helped establish the team as one of the most respected sides of its generation.

The club's greatest success came during HaxBall Champions League #7, when Pas De Nom captured its first and only title in a memorable final against Angels of Death.

Five editions later, Pas De Nom once again reached the championship match in HaxBall Champions League #12. The lineup of Tonnnek, Leu, Kranjcar and Tino gave the club a second opportunity to compete for the biggest title in European HaxBall.

The Art of the Dropball

Pas De Nom is widely recognized for introducing and perfecting one of the most influential mechanical innovations in competitive HaxBall: the Dropball Juke. While the basic idea of dropping the ball while in possession had been attempted by other players, Pas De Nom was among the first teams to integrate the technique into a broader collective philosophy.

By intentionally releasing control of the ball during a dribble, Pas De Nom created severe timing problems for opposing defenders. During the Flash era, when defensive positioning often depended on anticipating predictable movements, unexpected dropballs could disrupt defensive reactions and create passing opportunities. The technique became closely associated with the team and formed an important part of its tactical identity.

HaxBall Champions League History

Pas De Nom reached two HaxBall Champions League finals during its history, winning the competition in Edition #7 before reaching the championship match again in Edition #12.

The two campaigns featured different versions of the team but shared several important names, reflecting Pas De Nom's ability to remain competitive across multiple editions of the tournament.

HaxBall Champions League #7: The Road to the Final

The final of Edition #7 brought together two Turkish sides in a highly anticipated 4v4 match: Pas De Nom and Angels of Death. Angels of Death featured experienced players such as R9 and ROCKY BALBOA, who arrived at the final determined to claim the championship.

The final carried additional significance because of the existing hostility between the two teams. Of the eight players on the pitch, seven were Turkish and one was Portuguese. Prior to the match, Pas De Nom requested that the final be played on a Turkish host, but Angels of Death rejected the proposal. HaxBall Champions League administrators ultimately ruled that the match had to be played on a European host room.

Pas De Nom also played the final without Kranjcar, who had competed throughout the previous rounds but was unable to participate on the day of the final. Adékyz therefore joined the lineup as a replacement.

Another important tactical decision involved Tino. Normally known as one of the most distinguished strikers in HaxBall history, Tino was moved into midfield for the final as Pas De Nom sought to strengthen its control of the game.

HaxBall Champions League #7 Final: Pas De Nom vs Angels of Death

The final lineups were:

  • Pas De Nom: Guti.Haz, Leu, Tino, Adékyz
  • Angels of Death: Rai, R9, ROCKY BALBOA, Dark

First Half:
Pas De Nom made the stronger start. In the third minute, an intense spamming battle between Rai and Adékyz ended with Adékyz finding the goal and giving Pas De Nom a 1–0 lead. Another chaotic spamming battle between ROCKY BALBOA and Adékyz soon followed, with Adékyz once again emerging with the ball and scoring to make it 2–0.

Second Half:
Angels of Death responded with their passing game. A combination between R9 and ROCKY BALBOA eventually sent the ball into the path of Dark, who converted the chance and reduced the deficit to 2–1.

Pas De Nom quickly restored its two-goal advantage. Following a spamming exchange between Leu and R9, Leu found Tino in midfield. Tino immediately played the ball to Adékyz, effectively putting the chance on a plate for him. Adékyz completed his hat-trick and restored the 3–1 lead.

Pas De Nom then sealed the final on the counter-attack. Adékyz turned provider by assisting Tino, who finished the move to make it 4–1.

The final ended in a convincing 4–1 victory for Pas De Nom. For Angels of Death, the defeat brought their rivalry with Pas De Nom to a disappointing conclusion, while Pas De Nom secured the biggest championship in its history.

HaxBall Champions League #12: One Step from a Second Crown

Five editions after its championship run in Edition #7, Pas De Nom once again found itself competing at the final stage of the HaxBall Champions League.

The Edition #12 lineup consisted of Tonnnek, Leu, Kranjcar and Tino. Leu and Tino remained important figures from the club's earlier championship period, while Kranjcar returned to a HaxBall Champions League final after having missed the Edition #7 championship match.

Standing between Pas De Nom and a second European crown was Skins, who fielded Liéds, Gilbert, Elysion and Catrice.

The appearance gave Pas De Nom an opportunity to become a two-time HaxBall Champions League winner and added a second final to the club's competitive history.

HaxBall Champions League #12 Final: Pas De Nom vs Skins

The final lineups were:

  • Pas De Nom: Tonnnek, Leu, Kranjcar, Tino
  • Skins: Liéds, Gilbert, Elysion, Catrice

First Half:
Skins found the opening goal through a combination between Elysion and Catrice. Elysion played the ball into Catrice, who directed his finish into the corner to give Skins a 1–0 lead.

Second Half:
Shortly after the second half began, the same partnership produced another goal. Elysion once again found Catrice with a pass, and Catrice converted the opportunity to make it 2–0.

Catrice later added a third following a rebound. As the loose ball escaped behind the Pas De Nom defense, he moved onto it and finished the chance to complete his hat-trick.

Pas De Nom managed to pull one goal back during the second half, keeping the final alive as the match moved toward its closing moments.

The final action of the match came from another rebound. Catrice reacted first to the loose ball and converted once again, completing a four-goal performance.

Catrice's fourth goal came in the final seconds of the match and brought the game to an immediate end, with no further kick-off taking place. Skins won the final 4–1, leaving Pas De Nom one victory short of a second HaxBall Champions League title.

Edition #12 Final: Pas De Nom 1–4 Skins

Legacy

Pas De Nom's legacy is defined by its tactical innovation, influential players, and success in the HaxBall Champions League during the Flash era. The team's use of the Dropball Juke became one of the most recognizable elements of its identity and contributed to its reputation as a tactically innovative side.

The club's triumph in HaxBall Champions League #7 remains its defining competitive achievement. The final against Angels of Death combined a longstanding rivalry, unusual circumstances surrounding the host selection, tactical adjustments, and an emphatic 4–1 victory.

The final also highlighted the importance of adaptation. Adékyz, brought into the lineup as a replacement for Kranjcar, produced a hat-trick, while Tino shifted from his usual striker role into midfield and later scored the fourth goal.

Five editions later, Pas De Nom demonstrated its ability to remain among Europe's strongest teams by reaching another HaxBall Champions League final. The Edition #12 campaign brought Kranjcar back into the final lineup alongside Tonnnek, Leu and Tino, giving the club another opportunity to compete for the championship.

With two HaxBall Champions League finals and one championship, an influential tactical identity, and a connection to several important players of the Flash era, Pas De Nom remains an important and highly respected name in the history of competitive HaxBall.
