Angels of Death, commonly known as AoD, was a Turkish HaxBall team regarded as one of the most recognizable and historically important clubs in Turkish HaxBall during the Flash era.

The club became especially known through its rivalries with major early-era teams such as Silas Beavers and Pas De Nom. These were not simply regular competitive matchups; both rivalries became closely connected with AoD's identity and produced some of the most memorable confrontations of the early Turkish scene.

Its history, however, contained a major contradiction. While Angels of Death was successful in Turkish domestic leagues and remained highly regarded as a club, that success never translated into a major European championship.

AoD reached the HaxBall Champions League final twice, first against Silas Beavers in Edition #3 and later against Pas De Nom in Edition #7, but lost both championship matches. Those defeats eventually became such a significant part of the team's story that leading figures R9 and ROCKY BALBOA decided to leave the old identity behind and continue under a new name: Stiff Wind.

Overview

Angels of Death belonged to the first major generation of Turkish competitive HaxBall teams.

The club brought together a number of recognizable players from the early scene, with names such as R9, ROCKY BALBOA, Micran, TinermaniA, Chilavert, Fan, E.Hazard, Dude# and Rai becoming associated with different periods of AoD's history.

AoD built much of its reputation through domestic competition, where it established itself as a successful and highly recognizable Turkish team.

Its standing was not based only on trophies. The club's presence within the early scene, recognizable players and repeated involvement in high-level rivalries made Angels of Death one of the names most closely associated with the development of competitive Turkish HaxBall.

Turkish Scene

Angels of Death enjoyed considerably greater success within the Turkish domestic scene than it did in major European competition.

The team consistently carried the reputation of a strong Turkish club and became one of the best-known names of its generation. Its domestic performances helped build the status that later made its European failures particularly striking.

AoD could compete successfully over the course of domestic league campaigns, but when the club reached the largest international stage, it repeatedly fell short of turning that strength into a major championship.

This contrast became one of the defining features of Angels of Death's history: a powerful and respected domestic club whose major European record never matched its reputation.

Rivalries

The history of Angels of Death became closely linked with several of the strongest teams from the early Flash era.

Two rivalries became particularly important: Silas Beavers and Pas De Nom.

The rivalry with Silas Beavers developed into one of the most intense relationships of the early Turkish scene. The two clubs were not merely competing for league positions or tournament progression; meetings between them carried a level of hostility and pride that made victory over the other side particularly important.

Pas De Nom later became another major domestic rival. As with Silas Beavers, the rivalry eventually reached the largest possible stage when AoD faced Pas De Nom in a HaxBall Champions League final.

Both rivals ultimately stood between Angels of Death and the trophy. AoD reached championship matches against Silas Beavers in #3 and Pas De Nom in #7, but failed to win either final.

HaxBall Champions League

Angels of Death reached the HaxBall Champions League final twice, but never won the competition.

Its first opportunity came in Edition #3 against bitter rival Silas Beavers. The match became an extraordinary contest that lasted approximately 60 minutes.

Four editions later, AoD returned to the final against another domestic rival, Pas De Nom. The second opportunity also ended in defeat.

These two finals came to define the difference between AoD's reputation and its major trophy record. The club was clearly strong enough to repeatedly reach the final stage, but the championship itself remained out of reach.

HaxBall Champions League #3: A Long Night Between Giants

Angels of Death's first HaxBall Champions League final came in Edition #3 against Silas Beavers, one of its greatest rivals and another major force within the early Turkish HaxBall scene.

By the time the two teams met in the final, the relationship between them had developed far beyond an ordinary sporting rivalry. AoD and Silas Beavers had become bitter opponents, with matches between the clubs carrying considerable pride and tension. Defeating the other side mattered almost as much as the competition surrounding the match itself.

That made the HaxBall Champions League final an unusually important event. Two of the most prominent Turkish teams of the early era were now meeting with a European championship at stake. The result had the potential to influence not only the history of the tournament, but also the developing hierarchy and rivalries of Turkish competitive HaxBall.

Angels of Death entered the final with Chilavert, R9, Raki and Micran.

What followed was one of the longest and most demanding major matches of the early era. The final lasted approximately 60 minutes, far beyond the length normally associated with a HaxBall match.

Neither team simply disappeared under the pressure of the occasion. Both sides played well and continued to resist one another as the match stretched on. What began as a championship final gradually became an endurance battle between two clubs that were already desperate to defeat each other.

The extraordinary duration gave the match a very different character from a normal final. Every additional period increased the pressure, while the existing rivalry ensured that neither side wanted to be remembered as the team that finally gave way.

For the players involved, the contest had become more than a simple attempt to win a trophy. It represented a chance to establish superiority over a direct rival at a time when the identity of the Turkish competitive scene was still being formed.

After nearly an hour of competition, Silas Beavers ultimately found a way to win. In a match where both sides had been able to compete at a high level for an extraordinary length of time, Silas Beavers managed to survive the battle and secure the championship.

For Angels of Death, the defeat was particularly painful. AoD had not simply lost a final; it had lost a marathon championship match against one of the clubs it most wanted to defeat.

The final became an important chapter in the histories of both teams and helped establish the rivalry between Angels of Death and Silas Beavers as one of the memorable conflicts of early Turkish HaxBall. Its combination of rivalry, championship pressure and an extraordinary 60-minute duration made it a match that stood apart from the ordinary tournament encounters of its era.

Final Lineup (#3): Chilavert • R9 • Raki • Micran

HaxBall Champions League #7

The defeat against Silas Beavers would not be AoD's final opportunity. Four editions later, the club returned to the HaxBall Champions League final, this time against another bitter Turkish rival: Pas De Nom.

The match carried additional importance because both teams represented the Turkish scene. Their rivalry had already developed considerable tension before the championship match.

The final itself also became surrounded by disagreement. With seven of the eight players involved being Turkish, Pas De Nom requested that the match be played on a Turkish host. Angels of Death rejected the proposal, and HaxBall Champions League administrators ultimately ruled that the final would be played on a European host.

The final lineup for Angels of Death consisted of Rai, R9, ROCKY BALBOA and Dark.

Pas De Nom made the stronger start. Adékyz scored twice to give his team a 2–0 lead.

Angels of Death responded during the second half through its passing game. A combination between R9 and ROCKY BALBOA eventually sent the ball into the path of Dark, who converted the chance and reduced the deficit to 2–1.

For a moment, AoD had brought itself back into the final. Pas De Nom, however, quickly restored its two-goal advantage before later adding another goal.

Pas De Nom ultimately defeated Angels of Death 4–1. For AoD, the result meant another major final without a championship: two appearances on the biggest stage, two defeats against two of its most important rivals.

Final Lineup (#7): Rai • R9 • ROCKY BALBOA • Dark

The Stiff Wind Rebrand

The repeated failures on the HaxBall Champions League stage eventually changed the direction of the club.

Despite Angels of Death's domestic strength and respected status, the defeats against Silas Beavers and Pas De Nom created the sense that the AoD name had become tied to disappointment on the biggest stage.

The circumstances made the frustration particularly difficult to ignore. AoD had not been eliminated quietly in early rounds. It had reached the championship match twice, including an extraordinary 60-minute battle with Silas Beavers, yet still had no major European title to show for those runs.

Leading figures R9 and ROCKY BALBOA eventually decided that a complete fresh start was needed. The team moved away from the Angels of Death identity and adopted a new name: Stiff Wind.

The change represented an attempt to leave the club's major-final history behind and begin again without the weight attached to the AoD name.

The decision would become historically significant. Under the new Stiff Wind identity, R9 and ROCKY BALBOA finally broke through in HaxBall Champions League #9, defeating Antartic Lions 2–1 and winning the championship that had repeatedly escaped them during the AoD years.

Legacy

Angels of Death occupies an unusual place in Turkish HaxBall history.

The club is remembered as one of the strongest and most recognizable Turkish teams of the early competitive era, with a history built around domestic success, major rivalries and a roster containing many notable figures from the Flash generation.

Its rivalry with Silas Beavers became particularly important in shaping that reputation. Their extraordinary HaxBall Champions League #3 final brought two giants of the early Turkish scene together for a match lasting approximately 60 minutes, turning a championship final into one of the defining battles between the clubs.

The later rivalry with Pas De Nom added another chapter to the story. AoD once again reached the HaxBall Champions League final against a major Turkish rival, and once again left without the trophy.

That produced the central contradiction of Angels of Death's history. The club could defeat strong opponents, succeed domestically and establish itself as one of the most respected names in Turkish HaxBall, but two HaxBall Champions League finals produced two defeats and no championship.

The inability to complete the final step became such an important part of the AoD story that its leading figures eventually chose to abandon the name entirely. The transformation into Stiff Wind represented an attempt to escape the weight of the club's repeated major-final disappointment.

The success that followed under the new name gave the AoD period an even more unusual historical position. R9 and ROCKY BALBOA had been unable to win the HaxBall Champions League as Angels of Death, yet after beginning again as Stiff Wind, they finally became European champions.

Despite never lifting the HaxBall Champions League trophy, Angels of Death remained a highly regarded and deeply recognizable club. Its domestic success, historic rivalries with Silas Beavers and Pas De Nom, the marathon #3 final and its direct connection to the later rise of Stiff Wind ensured that AoD retained an important place in the history of Turkish HaxBall.
