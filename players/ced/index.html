Ced is a HaxBall striker from the early competitive period, remembered for combining individual attacking ability with an understanding of when to involve his teammates.

Rather than relying exclusively on either individual skill or combination play, Ced was capable of moving between the two. He could take responsibility when space appeared, but was also comfortable using teammates and allowing an attack to develop collectively.

His ability to read the pitch and identify gaps made him a consistent threat in attacking situations. With the ball, Ced could force defenders to react to him directly, creating danger both through his own actions and through the space his movement opened for others.

Ced also became an early example of a more modern interpretation of the striker position. During a period when advanced tricks, individual moves and powerful rocket shots were considerably less common at competitive level, he was already using those tools as part of his attacking game.

At club level, Ced reached two HaxBall Champions League finals. He first finished runner-up with Les 5 Fantastiques in Edition #4 before winning the competition with Hellhounds in Edition #6.

His championship final with Hellhounds became the most clearly documented major performance of his career. Ced scored two goals in a dominant 4–0 victory over Die Galaktischen.

Biography

Ced competed during an important period in the development of early competitive HaxBall, when individual mechanics and tactical ideas were still developing rapidly.

As a striker, his game was not based solely on remaining in advanced areas and waiting for finishing opportunities. Ced was capable of becoming involved with the ball, creating his own attacking situations and using individual ability to force openings.

At the same time, he retained a strong connection to team play. His decision-making allowed him to recognize when an individual action was available and when involving another player offered a better route toward goal.

This balance became one of the defining characteristics of his game and helped make him an effective attacking player during the early Flash period.

HaxBall Champions League Career

Ced reached the HaxBall Champions League final twice during his career.

His first final came in Edition #4 with Les 5 Fantastiques. Two editions later, he returned to the championship match with Hellhounds and became a HaxBall Champions League winner.

The two finals presented contrasting outcomes but established Ced as a recurring presence at the highest level of European competition during the period.

HaxBall Champions League #4: First Final

Ced reached his first HaxBall Champions League final in Edition #4 with Les 5 Fantastiques.

The final lineup consisted of pkp, Rooney, Razmo and Ced.

Their opponents were defending champions Silas Beavers, who lined up with SeRGeN, Dexter, NukroyS and Clinical Striker.

The match remained close, but Silas Beavers eventually won 1–0, leaving Ced and Les 5 Fantastiques as runners-up.

HaxBall Champions League #6: Championship Performance

Two editions after his first final, Ced returned to the championship match with Hellhounds.

Hellhounds lined up with Adriano, aguero10, Stingox and Ced. Their opponents were Die Galaktischen.

Hellhounds controlled the final and ultimately recorded a dominant 4–0 victory.

After an own goal opened the scoring, Ced doubled the advantage. aguero10 found Ced with a pass, and Ced finished into the corner to make it 2–0.

Ced struck again for the third goal. This time Stingox created the opportunity, with Ced once again finishing into the corner.

Ced therefore scored two of Hellhounds' four goals in the final, playing a central role in the victory that secured his first HaxBall Champions League championship.

Championship Lineup (#6): Adriano • aguero10 • Stingox • Ced
Final: Hellhounds 4–0 Die Galaktischen
Ced: 2 Goals
Result: 🏆 Champion

Playing Style

Ced played primarily as a striker and was characterized by a balance between individual attacking ability and combination play.

One of the strengths of his game was recognizing when an attack required individual initiative. When space was available, Ced could take responsibility himself, carry the attacking action forward and attempt to beat the defender directly.

However, his game was not based on constantly forcing individual actions. Ced was also capable of identifying when a teammate was in a better position and using passing combinations to continue the attack.

This ability to switch between individual and collective solutions made him difficult to predict. Defenders could not simply prepare for a shot, trick or pass because Ced could select between several options depending on the available space.

His reading of the pitch was particularly important. Ced could recognize gaps within a defensive structure and use those openings to create dangerous attacking situations either for himself or for his teammates.

His HaxBall Champions League #6 final provided a clear example of his finishing ability, with Ced converting two separate opportunities into goals during Hellhounds' championship victory.

An Early Example of the Modern Striker

Ced's playing style also represented an early version of what would later become more common among high-level HaxBall strikers.

During the early 2010s, many of the advanced individual mechanics that later became familiar within competitive HaxBall were still difficult to execute consistently. Tricks, quick individual actions and powerful rocket shots carried greater risk and were less routine than they would become in later generations.

Ced was already comfortable incorporating those elements into his attacking game.

When he received the ball, defenders had to respect the possibility of an immediate individual action. Ced could attempt to trick an opponent, create his own shooting angle or produce a powerful finish without requiring an extended team sequence beforehand.

At the same time, the presence of those individual tools did not remove the collective side of his game. He remained capable of combining with teammates and allowing the attack to develop through passing when that represented the better option.

This combination of individual threat, finishing, creativity and team play made Ced one of the early examples of the more complete striker profile that became increasingly common as competitive HaxBall evolved.

Legacy

Ced's competitive record includes two HaxBall Champions League final appearances and one championship.

His most notable documented major performance came in Edition #6, where he scored twice in Hellhounds' 4–0 victory over Die Galaktischen and became a HaxBall Champions League champion.

His historical relevance, however, is not limited to the championship itself. Ced represented an attacking profile that combined technical individuality with collective decision-making at a time when the competitive game was still developing many of its later conventions.

His willingness to use tricks, powerful shooting and direct individual actions made him dangerous with the ball, while his understanding of when to use teammates prevented his game from becoming dependent on individual play alone.

That balance places Ced among the early examples of a more modern and complete interpretation of the striker position in competitive HaxBall.
