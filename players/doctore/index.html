Doctore is a Turkish HaxBall player widely regarded as one of the greatest players in the game's history and one of the defining figures of early competitive HaxBall in Turkey.

He became best known as the captain and one of the central figures of Silas Beavers, a team regarded as one of the strongest and most important clubs of the early Flash era.

Under a core led by Doctore, Silas Beavers first established itself as a dominant force in Turkish HaxBall before carrying that success onto the international stage. The team became one of the strongest sides in the wider competitive scene and won consecutive HaxBall Champions League titles in Editions #3 and #4.

Doctore later added a second personal HaxBall Champions League championship in Edition #5, competing for Zebras under the name David Luiz.

Playing primarily as an AM, Doctore combined scoring, creativity and leadership. Contemporary players from his generation described him as the leading attacking midfielder of his time, while his influence extended beyond conventional play through his early use of trickshots and doubleshots.

Within the Turkish scene, Doctore became one of the players most closely associated with introducing and popularizing those techniques in serious competitive matches. Although trickshots and doubleshots cannot be definitively credited to a single inventor across HaxBall as a whole, Doctore was among the earliest players to use them consistently and prominently in Turkish competition.

Biography

Doctore belonged to the earliest major generation of competitive Turkish HaxBall players and established himself as one of the leading attacking players of the Flash era.

His reputation was built around more than individual technique. Doctore became an important figure during a period when Turkish competitive HaxBall was still developing its identity, with both his playing style and leadership helping shape one of the country's most successful early teams.

At AM, he was regarded by players from his generation as one of the strongest attacking midfielders of the period. His game combined direct scoring ability with creative passing, allowing him to influence matches both as a scorer and as a provider.

His career became inseparable from Silas Beavers, where he served as captain and became one of the central personalities behind the club's rise from domestic dominance to international success.

Silas Beavers

Doctore became most closely associated with Silas Beavers, one of the defining teams of early competitive HaxBall.

Under a core led by Doctore, the club first established itself as a dominant force in Turkish HaxBall. Silas Beavers became one of the strongest and most recognizable teams in the country, winning domestic competitions and developing major rivalries with other leading Turkish clubs.

That success did not remain limited to Turkey. Silas Beavers carried its dominance onto the international stage and became one of the strongest teams in the wider HaxBall scene.

The clearest demonstration came in the HaxBall Champions League, where Silas Beavers won back-to-back championships in Editions #3 and #4. The consecutive titles established the club as one of the earliest international powerhouses to emerge from Turkish HaxBall.

Doctore was not simply another member of the squad. He served as the captain and became one of the players most strongly associated with the identity, mentality and early success of the team.

His attacking influence and leadership gave him an unusual level of responsibility. While Silas Beavers featured several major players from the early era, Doctore stood at the center of the team's structure as both an attacking presence and a leader.

Captaincy and Leadership

Leadership became one of the defining parts of Doctore's reputation.

As captain of Silas Beavers, he carried responsibility during one of the strongest periods in the club's history. His role extended beyond his individual performance at AM, with contemporary players remembering him as one of the strongest leaders of the early competitive era.

Doctore's standing within the team was particularly important because Silas Beavers contained several high-level players. Leading a team with that level of individual quality required more than technical ability, and his captaincy became part of the reason his name remained closely tied to the club long after his playing period.

Former teammate and contemporary Clinical Striker later described Doctore as the anchor and captain of the legendary Silas Beavers side and emphasized his leadership as one of the strongest elements of his game.

HaxBall Champions League Career

Doctore competed in one of the formative periods of the HaxBall Champions League and became a two-time HaxBall Champions League champion.

His first championship came with Silas Beavers in Edition #3, where the club defeated Turkish rival Angels of Death after one of the longest and most memorable finals of the early era.

Silas Beavers also won the following edition, although Doctore was unavailable for the Edition #4 final. He later returned to the competition with Zebras in Edition #5, playing under the name David Luiz and winning his second personal Champions League title.

HaxBall Champions League #3: A Long Night Between Giants

Doctore won his first HaxBall Champions League championship with Silas Beavers in Edition #3.

The championship lineup consisted of TinerMania, Dexter, Doctore and Clinical Striker.

Their opponents were Angels of Death, another major Turkish club and one of Silas Beavers' fiercest rivals. By that point, both teams represented different sides of the emerging hierarchy of Turkish competitive HaxBall.

The final therefore carried importance beyond the European championship itself. Two of the strongest Turkish teams of the era were competing not only for the HaxBall Champions League trophy, but also for superiority within a rivalry that had become one of the defining conflicts of the early Turkish scene.

The match became extraordinary because of its duration. The final lasted approximately 60 minutes, with both teams continuing to resist one another through an unusually long and demanding contest.

Neither side disappeared as the match stretched further beyond normal playing time. What began as a championship final gradually became an endurance battle between two teams that had already developed a deep competitive rivalry.

Silas Beavers ultimately found a way to survive the marathon contest and secure the championship, giving the club its first HaxBall Champions League title.

For Doctore, the victory became one of the defining achievements of his career. As captain and one of the central attacking figures of Silas Beavers, he had helped lead a team that had already established itself in Turkey to the top of international HaxBall.

HaxBall Champions League #5: Champion with Zebras

Doctore returned to the HaxBall Champions League in Edition #5 as part of Zebras.

During this campaign, he competed under the nickname David Luiz.

The Zebras championship lineup consisted of Иukroyš, Dexter, Doctore (David Luiz) and Kranjcar.

Zebras went on to win HaxBall Champions League #5, giving Doctore his second personal Champions League championship.

The victory also continued the success of the generation closely associated with Silas Beavers and completed Dexter's third consecutive HaxBall Champions League title.

International Career

Doctore also represented Turkey in international competition during the early Flash era.

Nations Cup 2013 - Big Easy

Doctore reached the final of Nations Cup 2013 - Big Easy with Turkey.

Turkey lined up with Lev Yashin, barya, Whiplash and Doctore, while Portugal fielded Markogol, Wigas, Tortogol and Dark.

Playing Style

Doctore played primarily as an AM and was regarded as one of the outstanding attacking midfielders of the early Flash era.

His attacking game was built around a combination of scoring ability, passing and technical creativity. Rather than contributing in only one area, he could influence a match through both direct finishing and chance creation.

Before the formation of Silas Beavers, Doctore had been a more individually oriented player. As the team began to take shape, he deliberately looked to add new dimensions to his game, including dropping deeper more frequently to support defensively and placing greater emphasis on passing and combination play. This evolution helped bring his individual qualities into the more collective style that would eventually define Silas Beavers.

Contemporary accounts from the Turkish scene describe Doctore as the leading AM of his period, with his ability to combine goals and assists making him one of the main attacking references of the Silas Beavers team.

The most recognizable part of his style, however, was his willingness to attempt techniques that were still unusual in serious competitive matches.

Trickshots and Doubleshots

Doctore became particularly famous within Turkish HaxBall for his use of trickshots and doubleshots.

The exact global origin of these techniques cannot be assigned with certainty to one player, and similar ideas may have been used independently elsewhere. Doctore's importance came from how early and consistently he used them within the Turkish competitive scene.

He was among the first Turkish players known to attempt these actions regularly rather than treating them as occasional experiments. More importantly, he was willing to use them in important competitive matches, helping move the techniques from novelty into serious play.

For that reason, Doctore became one of the players most strongly associated with the early introduction and spread of trickshots and doubleshots in Turkish HaxBall.

Not every later player necessarily learned these techniques directly from Doctore, but his repeated use of them helped demonstrate what was possible and contributed to their growing visibility within the Turkish scene.

Scoring and Creativity

Doctore's reputation at Silas Beavers was not built only on style or leadership. He was also remembered as one of the team's most productive attacking players.

According to contemporary accounts from players of the era, Doctore maintained both the scoring record and assist record within the legendary Silas Beavers team.

Those records reflected the balance in his game as an AM. He was capable of finishing attacks himself while also creating opportunities for the players around him, making him central to the team's offensive structure.

This combination of production and creativity helped establish his reputation as one of the best attacking players of his generation.

Legacy

Doctore holds a distinctive place in the history of early competitive HaxBall.

He is remembered as one of the greatest players in HaxBall history, one of the leading AMs of his generation and one of the central figures behind the rise of Silas Beavers.

His importance was closely tied to the scale of that rise. Silas Beavers first established dominance within Turkish HaxBall before carrying the same competitive strength onto the international stage, where consecutive HaxBall Champions League championships placed the club among the leading teams of its era.

As captain, Doctore stood at the center of that transformation. His status was built from several different elements: leadership, attacking production, creativity and the responsibility of guiding one of the strongest early teams in the game.

His competitive record also extended beyond Silas Beavers. His championship with Zebras in HaxBall Champions League #5, where he competed under the name David Luiz, gave him a second Champions League title, while his appearance in the Nations Cup 2013 - Big Easy final added a major international final with Turkey.

His technical influence was equally important within Turkey. Doctore became one of the earliest prominent users of trickshots and doubleshots in serious competition, helping introduce and normalize techniques that would later become more familiar to Turkish players.

The combination of leadership, creativity, scoring and innovation made him more than simply a successful player from the Flash era. For the Turkish scene in particular, Doctore became one of the figures who helped define what high-level attacking HaxBall could look like during the game's formative competitive years.

His role as captain of Silas Beavers, his two HaxBall Champions League championships, his reputation as one of the strongest AMs of his time and his influence on early Turkish attacking play ensured that Doctore remained one of the most important names in early HaxBall history.
