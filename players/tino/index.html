Tino is a Turkish HaxBall striker regarded as one of the most creative strikers in HaxBall history. A Flash-era player whose competitive career was relatively short, Tino nevertheless left an impression that extended far beyond the length of time he spent at the highest level.

His reputation was built around the individuality of his game. Tino became remembered for his creativity, unusual attacking ideas and memorable goals, developing a style that made him one of the most recognizable strikers of the early competitive era.

His club career was entirely associated with Pas De Nom. Like his close friend Leu, Tino did not build a career across different clubs, instead remaining connected to Pas De Nom throughout his time in competitive HaxBall.

With Pas De Nom, Tino won HaxBall Champions League #7 and later returned to the final in #12. Beyond those results, he became one of the most recognizable attacking figures of the Flash era through a playing style that was admired for its creativity and originality. His name also became closely identified with Pas De Nom, the only club he represented during his competitive career.

Biography

Tino emerged during the Flash era and quickly became known as an unusually creative striker.

Although primarily a striker, his reputation was not based simply on scoring goals. The way he approached attacking situations, the solutions he found around defenders and the individuality of his play became central parts of how he was remembered.

Tino's competitive career did not last as long as those of many players who would later build reputations across several eras of HaxBall. His entire career belonged to Flash, and he stepped away before the game entered its later HTML5 period.

Despite that relatively short period, Tino established a lasting reputation. His style and memorable goals remained part of the collective memory of early competitive HaxBall, giving his career an influence that was greater than its length might suggest.

Pas De Nom and Leu

Tino's club identity was inseparable from Pas De Nom.

Unlike many successful players who moved between several major teams, Tino spent his known club career entirely with Pas De Nom. Both his greatest competitive achievements and the style for which he became remembered were developed under the same club name.

A particularly important relationship within the team was his friendship with Leu. The two were close friends and followed remarkably similar club careers: neither played for another club during their known competitive careers.

This made Tino and Leu especially closely connected with Pas De Nom. Rather than becoming associated with several teams over time, both players became part of the identity of one club.

Pas De Nom itself became known during the Flash era for an inventive attacking identity, particularly through its use of Dropball Jukes. Tino's creativity fitted naturally within a team that placed unusual technical ideas at the center of its approach.

Over time, Tino became one of the players most strongly identified with Pas De Nom. The connection between player and club remained an important part of how both were remembered after the Flash era.

HaxBall Champions League Career

Tino reached the HaxBall Champions League final twice with Pas De Nom.

The first ended with a championship in Edition #7, when Pas De Nom defeated Angels of Death. Five editions later, Tino and Leu were again part of the team that reached the final in #12.

Those two campaigns produced a record of one championship and one runner-up finish, with Tino remaining under the Pas De Nom banner in both appearances.

HaxBall Champions League #7

Tino's greatest competitive achievement came in HaxBall Champions League #7.

Pas De Nom reached the final against Angels of Death, another Turkish team featuring players including R9 and ROCKY BALBOA.

The final also forced Pas De Nom to make changes. Kranjcar, who had played during the earlier rounds, was unavailable for the championship match and was replaced by Adékyz.

Tino was normally used as a striker, but for the final he was moved into midfield as Pas De Nom adjusted its structure for the match.

Pas De Nom built a 2–0 lead through two Adékyz goals before Angels of Death reduced the deficit to 2–1.

Tino then became directly involved in one of the decisive moves of the final. After Leu found Tino in midfield, Tino immediately created the opportunity for Adékyz, who completed his hat-trick and restored Pas De Nom's two-goal advantage at 3–1.

The roles were reversed for the final goal. Adékyz provided the pass and Tino finished the counter-attack, scoring the goal that completed a 4–1 victory.

Tino therefore ended the biggest match of Pas De Nom's history with both a decisive creative contribution and the final goal of the championship.

Championship Lineup (#7): Guti.Haz • Leu • Tino • Adékyz

HaxBall Champions League #12

Five editions after the championship, Tino returned to the HaxBall Champions League final with Pas De Nom.

The Edition #12 lineup consisted of tonnnek, Leu, Kranjcar and Tino. Tino and Leu remained from the club's earlier championship period, while Kranjcar returned to the lineup after missing the #7 final.

Their opponents were Skins, another Turkish team, who fielded Liéds, Gilbert, Elysion and Catrice. Just as in the #7 final against Angels of Death, Pas De Nom once again found itself facing another Turkish side on the biggest stage of the HaxBall Champions League.

Skins established control of the final through Catrice, who produced a four-goal performance. Pas De Nom managed to score once during the second half, but could not complete a comeback.

Catrice scored his fourth goal in the final seconds of the match, immediately bringing the game to an end. Skins won 4–1, leaving Tino and Pas De Nom one victory short of a second HaxBall Champions League championship.

Final Lineup (#12): tonnnek • Leu • Kranjcar • Tino

Playing Style

Creativity was the defining feature of Tino's game.

As a striker, he was remembered for finding unusual solutions in attacking situations and for producing moments that were difficult for opponents to anticipate. His style gave his performances an individuality that remained recognizable even within the highly creative Pas De Nom team.

His reputation was also closely connected with the goals he scored. Rather than being remembered only through statistics, much of the appreciation surrounding Tino came from the manner in which he played and finished attacking moves.

His attacking style was admired for its imagination and originality. The unpredictability of his decisions allowed him to produce moments that remained memorable even after his active career had ended.

The HaxBall Champions League #7 final also showed another side of his game. Although naturally a striker, Tino was able to move into midfield for the championship match and still contribute directly to both chance creation and scoring.

That combination of attacking imagination and adaptability helped establish Tino as one of the most creative strikers of the Flash era.

Short Career

Tino's time at the highest level was relatively short.

His career took place entirely during the Flash era, and he did not continue into the later HTML5 generation.

The limited length of his career, however, did not prevent him from becoming one of the memorable names of his period. His style was sufficiently individual that his reputation survived far beyond the seasons in which he actively competed.

For Tino, longevity was never the defining part of the story. His legacy was instead created through impact, creativity and memorable moments.

Legacy

Tino's legacy is unusual because of how much recognition he achieved within a relatively short competitive career.

He did not need a long list of clubs or multiple eras of competition to establish his identity. His career remained entirely tied to Pas De Nom, where his friendship and long association with Leu became part of the team's story.

Few players became as closely identified with a single club as Tino did with Pas De Nom. Together with his close friend Leu, he spent his entire known club career under the Pas De Nom name, making both players an inseparable part of the team's identity.

His HaxBall Champions League record provided the competitive foundation of that legacy. Tino became champion in #7, contributing both creatively and as a goalscorer in the final, before returning to the championship match in #12.

Yet trophies alone do not fully explain how Tino is remembered. He was remembered above all for the way he played. His creativity, unusual attacking ideas and memorable goals made his style particularly admired among those who watched him during the Flash era.

The connection with Pas De Nom became equally important. His style developed within one of the most inventive teams of the early scene, and in return Tino became one of the players most closely associated with the club's identity.

Despite leaving competitive HaxBall after a comparatively brief career, Tino left a lasting impression through the individuality of his game. His creativity, memorable goals and deep association with Pas De Nom remain central to his legacy, and he continues to be remembered as one of the most creative strikers HaxBall has produced.
