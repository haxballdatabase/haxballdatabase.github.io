Guti.Haz is a HaxBall goalkeeper from the Flash era who was regarded as one of the leading goalkeepers of his generation. He is best known for his time with Pas De Nom, where he became part of the side that won HaxBall Champions League #7.

He was particularly notable for his proactive and pressure-oriented goalkeeping style. Rather than remaining close to his own goal and waiting for attacks to develop, Guti.Haz was willing to step forward, reduce space and pressure opposing players earlier.

Biography

Guti.Haz emerged during the early Flash era and established himself among the most prominent goalkeepers of his period.

His career became most closely associated with Pas De Nom, where he played during one of the club's most successful periods.

Pas De Nom

Guti.Haz was part of the Pas De Nom team that became one of the notable competitive sides of the early era.

His most important success with the club came in HaxBall Champions League #7, when Pas De Nom won the championship.

A Proactive Goalkeeping Style

One of the characteristics that distinguished Guti.Haz was his willingness to play more aggressively than many goalkeepers of the period.

Instead of remaining permanently close to the goal line, he frequently moved forward to close down opponents, reduce available space and apply pressure before attacks could fully develop.

Within Turkish competitive HaxBall, Guti.Haz was among the early goalkeepers associated with this more proactive approach. His style represented an early example of the pressure-oriented goalkeeping that would become increasingly familiar in later periods of the game.

HaxBall Champions League #7

Guti.Haz became a HaxBall Champions League champion with Pas De Nom in Edition #7.

Pas De Nom lined up with Guti.Haz, Leu, Tino and Adékyz in the final against Angels of Death, winning the championship with a 4–1 victory.

Legacy

Guti.Haz is remembered as one of the leading goalkeepers of his generation, combining major success with a goalkeeping style that stood out during the early competitive era.

His willingness to defend proactively and pressure opponents further away from goal made him one of the notable early examples of this style among goalkeepers in the Turkish HaxBall scene.
